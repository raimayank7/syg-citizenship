About Citizenship Test

Citizenship Test is a free study site for the civics part of the U.S. naturalization test. It carries both official question lists, the 2025 version (128 questions) and the 2008 version (100 questions), with a short plain note on every question, a spoken-style practice test, flashcards and guides to how the test works.

Independent, not official

This site is not a government website. It is not connected with, approved by or paid by U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS), the Department of Homeland Security or any other agency. We do not file applications, give legal advice or know anything about your case. The official source for everything about naturalization is uscis.gov/citizenship, and its study materials are free.

Where the questions and rules come from

The questions and their accepted answers are quoted from the official USCIS lists, which are works of the U.S. government and in the public domain. The rules of the test, the exceptions and the list of current office holders were read on these official pages on 12 November 2026:

  • 128 Civics Questions and Answers (2025 version), form M-1778 (09/25)
  • Civics Questions and Answers (2008 version), rev. 08/21
  • USCIS: Study for the Test
  • USCIS: Check for Test Updates (marked last reviewed 09/18/2025)
  • USCIS: Exceptions and Accommodations (marked last reviewed 01/09/2026)
  • USCIS Policy Manual, Volume 12, Part E, Chapter 2: English and Civics Testing

What is ours

The note under each question, the introductions to each part, the guides, the study plan and the practice tools are our own writing and work. The notes state settled facts of U.S. history and government. They explain an answer; they are not themselves official answers. In the test, give an answer from the official list.

Keeping it current

Names of office holders change. We show what the USCIS test updates page listed on the date above and tell you to check that page yourself. We do not list senators, representatives or governors.

Other countries

For now the site covers the United States test only.
